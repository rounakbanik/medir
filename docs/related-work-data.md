# Related-work data comparison

This comparison separates source overlap from evaluation comparability. A shared source does
not mean the papers use the same snapshot, labels, task, or scoring unit. Counts below are those
reported by the cited papers and should not be read as directly comparable corpus sizes.

| Work | Literature and reference data | Labels / evaluation target | Relationship to `medir` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`medir` (this repository)** | PubMed abstracts sampled by disease/query; openFDA's full Drugs@FDA bulk export; B3DB BBB classifications; archived repoDB. See [source snapshots](sources.md). | RepoDB drug–disease indication outcomes are used for indication recovery; they are kept separate from searchable text. Ranking is evaluated at the drug/entity level. FDA application data supplies approval evidence, and B3DB supplies a BBB feature. | The baseline and expanded PubMed samples use the same configured disease scope and query filters, with different per-query limits. Drugs@FDA is an explicit source in this pipeline; it is not interchangeable with any paper's generic list of FDA-approved drugs. |
| **DrugReX** | Roughly 30 million PubMed records and 5.6 million PMC full-text articles are processed with SemMedDB/SemRep into a literature knowledge graph. | Reports knowledge-graph prediction evaluations and case studies, including 15 established repurposing cases and an Alzheimer's-related use case. | Shares biomedical literature and FDA-related case context, but its full-text graph corpus and knowledge-graph evaluation are not a direct baseline for scoped abstract retrieval. The paper does not establish use of the same openFDA Drugs@FDA bulk export. ([paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12204371/)) |
| **KEDRec-LM** | Uses DRKG to form drug–disease candidate examples; retrieves from PMC and ClinicalTrials.gov, reporting a filtered corpus of about 1.9 million articles. MIMIC-III is also reported as a separate evaluation resource. | Candidate labels are derived from DRKG relationships; evaluation includes drug-selection and explanation tasks. | Literature retrieval overlaps conceptually, but the candidate labels and selection/generation task differ from `medir`'s RepoDB-labeled entity ranking. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20350)) |
| **Deep Learning-Based Drug Repurposing Using KG Embeddings and GraphRAG** | Uses DRKG for graph embeddings and GraphRAG explanations. | The accessible paper abstract describes graph-based prediction and explanation; it is insufficient to verify an independently judged text-retrieval benchmark. | Related to repurposing, but the reported data and task do not support a direct retrieval-score comparison with `medir`. ([paper abstract](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.08.693009v1)) |
| **Liang et al., Literature data-based de novo candidates for drug repurposing** | Builds a drug–target literature network and uses RepoDB in validation; its analysis includes FDA-approved/investigational drugs. | RepoDB relationships are used to form drug-pair examples based on shared approved indications, then evaluated as a classification/similarity task. This is not the same unit as a drug–disease query label. | RepoDB is a clear source overlap, and FDA-related drug data appear in both lines of work. However, the paper's drug-pair labels cannot be substituted for `medir`'s drug–disease relevance judgments; its FDA drug list is not documented as the same openFDA bulk snapshot. ([paper](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12859-025-06237-7)) |
| **Moreau, Literature-based discovery: addressing the issue of the subpar evaluation methodology** | Methodology discussion, not a new drug-repurposing corpus. It reviews small replication sets and time-sliced literature co-occurrence evaluation. | Argues that small discovery sets can be biased and that time-sliced co-occurrences are numerous but noisy proxies for genuine discoveries. | Informs how we state limitations: RepoDB supports indication-recovery benchmarking, but it does not by itself prove a novel or clinically validated repurposing discovery. ([paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9945845/)) |

## Comparison takeaways

- **Sources overlap:** PubMed is used in several systems; RepoDB is shared with Liang et al.; FDA-related drug information appears in more than one line of work. `medir` should not claim novelty from simply combining familiar sources.
- **Drugs@FDA has a specific role here:** it is an explicit approval-evidence source in `medir`. A paper that starts with FDA-approved drugs may use a different drug list and does not establish use of the Drugs@FDA bulk export.
- **The evaluation units differ:** `medir` ranks drugs for a disease (and diseases for a drug); other works evaluate graph links, selected candidates, explanations, or drug–drug pairs. Their reported metrics should not be compared as if they scored the same task.
- **Snapshot expansion is a coverage change:** retain the documented 100-per-disease initial baseline and its counts, then record the actual selected counts and unique abstracts from the expanded 500-per-disease acquisition manifests. Do not commit raw abstracts unless their redistribution terms permit it.
